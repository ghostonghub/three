A
Introduction
  • Cross-dressing is legal in India and is not considered a crime, as the Supreme Court has recognized transgender individuals as a "third gender" with the right to self-identify, protecting gender expression under the law. However, social acceptance varies widely, with cross-dressing often celebrated in specific religious rituals and festivals but facing stigma or discrimination in general public spaces due to prevailing social norms.
Legal and Social Context
  • Legality: It is not an offense unless it involves obscene acts or public indecency under the IPC. The Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act, 2019 further safeguards individuals against harassment and forced separation from family.
  • Cultural Traditions: Historical and religious practices include cross-dressing, such as Launda Naach in Uttar Pradesh and the Chamayavilakku festival in Kerala, where men dress as women for ritualistic purposes.
  • Modern Reality: While urban hubs like Mumbai and Delhi offer safer, LGBTQ+ inclusive spaces for expression, many cross-dressers remain closeted in public to avoid prejudice, often confining their expression to private settings or specific community events.

  • Cross Dressing In Mahabharat
A
Introduction
  • Cross-dressing and gender fluidity are significant themes in the Mahabharata, serving both as strategic disguises and narratives of gender identity. The most prominent examples involve the warriors Arjuna and Shikhandi.
Arjuna’s Disguise as Brihannala
  • During the Pandavas' final year of exile (Agyatvas), Arjuna was cursed by the celestial nymph Urvashi to lose his manhood. To maintain his anonymity in the court of King Virata, Arjuna adopted the persona of Brihannala, a eunuch and mistress of dance. In this role, he cross-dressed as a woman, wearing silks and jewelry, and taught music and dance to the princess Uttara. This disguise allowed him to remain incognito while protecting the Pandava family until the end of their exile.
Shikhandi’s Gender Transformation
  • The character of Shikhandi (also known as Shikhandini) represents a profound narrative of gender transition. Born female to King Drupada, Shikhandi was raised as a boy to fulfill a prophecy that she would kill Bhishma. After her biological sex was discovered on her wedding night, she fled to the forest and met a yaksha named Sthunakarna, who exchanged sexes with her. Shikhandini became Shikhandi, a trans-male warrior. During the Kurukshetra War, Bhishma refused to fight Shikhandi, respecting them as a woman in origin, which allowed Arjuna to defeat and mortally wound Bhishma.
Other Instances of Gender Fluidity
  • Lord Krishna: Often depicted engaging in gender-fluid behavior, such as wearing Radha’s earrings and garments, or taking the feminine form of Mohini to enchant demons and distribute the nectar of immortality.
  • Samba: Krishna’s son, who cross-dressed as a woman to prank sages, leading to a curse that ultimately contributed to the destruction of the Yadu clan.
  • Bhima: Disguised himself as a woman to punish the demon Kichaka, who had harassed Draupadi.
  • Ardhanarishvara: While primarily from the Puranas, this half-male, half-female form of Shiva symbolizes the transcendence of gender duality, a concept underlying many Mahabharata narratives.
EndNotes, Misc
  • These stories illustrate that gender identity and expression in the Mahabharata were viewed as porous and multifaceted, often serving divine or strategic purposes rather than being strictly bound by binary norms.
